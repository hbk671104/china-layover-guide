## Why Beijing works for a layover

Beijing is China's imperial headline act, and for many travelers flying between Europe, North America, and Asia it is the highest-impact stopover on the map. It is **not the easiest short layover**: both airports sit well outside the historic core, immigration queues can stack when long-haul flights land together, and the top sights require advance planning.

With **8 to 10 hours** you can see one major downtown landmark properly. With **12 hours** you get a full imperial day (the Forbidden City, Jingshan Park, a hutong walk, and Peking duck). With **10+ hours and a morning arrival** or an overnight stop, you can make a realistic run to the **Mutianyu Great Wall**.

## PEK vs. PKX: which airport changes your plan

Before picking a route or a hotel, check the three-letter airport code on both legs of your itinerary. Beijing's two international hubs sit on opposite sides of the city, roughly 80 km apart:

- **[Beijing Capital International Airport (PEK)](/airports/pek/)** sits ~25 km northeast of the center and is the better hub for short layovers. The Airport Express reaches Dongzhimen and Sanyuanqiao in 25–35 minutes for ¥25, and Mutianyu Great Wall is roughly 1.5 hours away by car.
- **[Beijing Daxing International Airport (PKX)](/airports/pkx/)** sits ~50 km south of the center. The Daxing Airport Express reaches Caoqiao in ~20 minutes for ¥35, where you switch to Metro Line 10 or 19. Add 1 to 2 extra hours to any sightseeing threshold compared with PEK, and expect ~2.5 hours each way to Mutianyu.
- **Cross-airport connections (PEK ↔ PKX)** take 1.5 to 2 hours across Beijing by airport bus or rail with two changes. Treat that transfer as the entire layover unless you have well over 12 hours.

## Choosing your route by layover length

- **Under 7 hours:** stay airside. Immigration, baggage, transfers, and the 2.5-hour international return buffer leave no usable city time.
- **8–10 hours:** pick one downtown cluster, either Tiananmen Square plus a hutong walk near the Drum Tower, or the Temple of Heaven. Do not try to chain sights across town.
- **12 hours:** pre-book the **Forbidden City** (enter from the south gate, exit north straight into **Jingshan Park** for the panoramic roof view), then sit down for Peking duck before taking the Airport Express back. See the full [Beijing 6, 8, 12, and 24-hour layover itinerary](/guides/beijing-layover-itinerary/) for exact timing math.
- **Morning 10+ hours or 24-hour overnight (Great Wall):** head straight to **Mutianyu** with a pre-booked private driver so someone is waiting for your return run. Read our [Great Wall from a Beijing layover guide](/guides/great-wall-from-beijing-layover/) before committing, especially if you land at PKX.

## Where to stay: airport hotel vs. downtown

If your connection is overnight or exceeds 14 hours, first check whether your airline covers your room for free. **Air China** (ticket prefix 999), **China Southern**, and **China Eastern** all run free transit hotel schemes at Beijing for qualifying through-tickets, though every carrier requires advance booking rather than walk-up requests. Check exact booking classes and deadlines in our [China airline free transit hotel guide](/guides/china-airline-layover-hotel/).

If you do not qualify for an airline hotel:

- **Short overnight (landing after 22:00 or departing before 09:00):** stay beside PEK or PKX to avoid late-night transfers after the Airport Express stops around 23:00.
- **Full 18–24 hour layover:** book downtown near **Dongzhimen, Qianmen, or Wangfujing** if flying out of PEK (direct Airport Express return), or near **Line 19 / Caoqiao** if flying out of PKX. Hotels automatically handle mandatory [foreigner accommodation registration](/guides/hotel-registration-for-foreigners-in-china/) at check-in.

## Practical logistics on the ground

- **Entry permission:** both PEK and PKX are designated ports under China's [240-hour visa-free transit policy](/transit-visa/) (covering Beijing municipality and cross-province permitted areas), and eligible passports on the 30-day visa-free list can enter without the third-country rule. Check [can you leave the airport during a China layover?](/guides/can-you-leave-the-airport-during-a-china-layover/) before booking.
- **Luggage:** manual left-luggage counters at PEK typically cost ¥30–40 per rolling suitcase per 24 hours, while PKX's landside manual counters are free. See [luggage storage at China's airports](/guides/luggage-storage-at-china-airports/) for current options.
- **Getting around:** the metro is traffic-proof and signed in English. For late arrivals, luggage, or a hotel transfer, set up [DiDi ride-hailing](/guides/didi-and-ride-hailing-in-china/) inside Alipay or WeChat so fares are upfront and addresses translate automatically.
- **Payments and connectivity:** install a travel eSIM before flying ([best eSIM for China](/guides/best-esim-for-china/)) and link a foreign card to both Alipay and [WeChat Pay](/guides/wechat-pay-and-tenpaygo-for-foreigners/). If you land without data, both Beijing airports support passport-based login on [China airport Wi-Fi](/guides/china-airport-wifi/).

## Food and local traps

If you have time for one proper meal, go for **Peking duck** or a fast bowl of **zhajiangmian** (fried bean sauce noodles) near the hutongs rather than snack stalls on Wangfujing. Two traps catch first-timers most often: **Forbidden City tickets are passport-linked and sell out days ahead** (do not expect walk-up tickets), and both Airport Express lines close around **23:00**, leaving late arrivals dependent on taxis or DiDi.
