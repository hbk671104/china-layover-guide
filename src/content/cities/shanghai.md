## Why Shanghai works for a layover

Shanghai is the smoothest first-time layover in mainland China. The rail links from both airports are predictable, English signage across the metro is clear, and the classic central sights sit close enough together that an 8-hour stop delivers a genuine half-day in the city rather than a sprint across suburbs.

Most international transits land at **Pudong (PVG)**, ~30 km east of downtown, while regional flights from Japan, South Korea, Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan often use **Hongqiao (SHA)**, only ~13 km west of the center. That airport split is the first thing to check before planning your hours.

## PVG vs. SHA: choosing the right transfer

- **[Shanghai Pudong International Airport (PVG)](/airports/pvg/)** handles almost all long-haul international connections. The fastest traffic-proof way in is the **Maglev** (8 minutes to Longyang Road, ¥50 or ¥40 at the counter with a same-day boarding pass) followed by **Metro Line 2** into Nanjing East Road or People's Square (~40–55 minutes total). Metro Line 2 direct costs ¥7–9 and takes 60–90 minutes.
- **[Shanghai Hongqiao International Airport (SHA)](/airports/sha/)** is the easiest layover airport in China: Metro Lines 2 and 10 connect directly from the terminal to downtown in 30–50 minutes for ¥3–7, making even a 5–6 hour stop workable.
- **Cross-airport connections (PVG ↔ SHA)** cover roughly 70 km. The Airport Link Line connects the two terminals in about 40 minutes (last train from Pudong at 22:15), but once you add immigration and a second check-in, do not plan sightseeing on a two-airport transfer under 12 hours.

## Planning by layover window

- **Under 6 hours from PVG:** stay airside. Arrivals immigration can take 30 to 60+ minutes in the evening long-haul wave, leaving no safe margin once you include the 2.5-hour return buffer.
- **8 hours from PVG:** take Maglev + Line 2 to Nanjing East Road, walk to **the Bund** for the Pudong skyline, grab a basket of *xiaolongbao* (soup dumplings), and head back on rail.
- **12 hours:** combine the Bund, **Yu Garden** and the old town, a walk along Nanjing Road, and either Lujiazui or the **Former French Concession**. See our hour-by-hour [Shanghai layover itinerary (6, 8, 12 & 24 hours)](/guides/shanghai-layover-itinerary/) for exact timing math and whether a layover tour makes sense.
- **24 hours+:** stay overnight in Puxi, add an evening river cruise, and consider a morning side trip to Zhujiajiao water town or a one-hour high-speed train to Hangzhou.

## Where to stay: airport hotel vs. Puxi overnight

Before booking a room yourself, check if your airline provides a complimentary stay. **[China Eastern](/guides/china-eastern-free-transit-hotel/)** (ticket prefix 781) and **[Air China](/guides/air-china-free-transit-hotel/)** (prefix 999) both offer free transit hotels at Shanghai for qualifying tickets, with strict advance-booking rules and fare-class exclusions. Verify your eligibility in our [China airline free transit hotel guide](/guides/china-airline-layover-hotel/).

If you are booking your own hotel, compare PVG airside sleep pods, Aerotel T2, and Da Zhong Airport Hotel in our [Shanghai Pudong Airport (PVG) layover hotels guide](/guides/shanghai-pudong-airport-layover-hotels/):

- **Late landing or early flight out of PVG:** stay inside or directly beside Pudong Airport so you are not racing early-morning traffic or arriving after the last Maglev (21:42) and Metro Line 2 (~22:30).
- **18–24 hour stopover:** stay along **Metro Line 2** in Puxi (**People's Square, Nanjing East Road, or Jing'an**). You can step out for dinner and the Bund at night and take a single direct rail line back to PVG or SHA the next day. Hotels register foreign guests automatically under China's [24-hour accommodation registration rule](/guides/hotel-registration-for-foreigners-in-china/).

## Practical logistics on the ground

- **Entry rules:** both PVG and SHA are designated ports under the [240-hour visa-free transit policy](/transit-visa/). Confirm whether you qualify under 240-hour transit or 30-day visa-free entry in [can you leave the airport during a China layover?](/guides/can-you-leave-the-airport-during-a-china-layover/).
- **Luggage:** Pudong has staffed left-luggage counters in arrivals and departures (~¥15–30 per bag per day). Details and alternatives are in [luggage storage at China's airports](/guides/luggage-storage-at-china-airports/).
- **Ride-hailing & payments:** after 22:30, rail stops and you will need a taxi or [DiDi](/guides/didi-and-ride-hailing-in-china/) (pick-ups at PVG are on level B2 in P2 car park, not arrivals kerb). Set up Alipay and [WeChat Pay](/guides/wechat-pay-and-tenpaygo-for-foreigners/) before flying, plus a travel eSIM ([best eSIM for China](/guides/best-esim-for-china/)) backed up by passport-login [China airport Wi-Fi](/guides/china-airport-wifi/).

## Food and local traps

Prioritize **soup dumplings (*xiaolongbao*)**, **scallion oil noodles**, and pan-fried **shengjianbao** over expensive tourist restaurants right on the Bund waterfront. Watch two hard cutoffs at PVG: the **last Maglev departs at 21:42** (every 20 minutes), and **Metro Line 2 closes around 22:30**. Also remember the Maglev stops at Longyang Road rather than downtown, so always factor in the Line 2 transfer.
