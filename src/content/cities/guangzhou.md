## Why Guangzhou works for a layover

Guangzhou is one of the busiest transit hubs for travelers flying between Europe, Australia, New Zealand, Southeast Asia, and Africa, largely thanks to **China Southern Airlines**. Unlike Beijing or Xi'an, Guangzhou is not a race to tick off one distant monument: it is China's culinary capital, warm most of the year, and connected directly to the center by a cheap, traffic-proof metro line.

The single rule that separates a great Guangzhou layover from a mediocre one is **arrival time**. Cantonese *yum cha* (dim sum) is a morning and lunch ritual. If you land in the morning, eat dim sum first before doing anything else.

## Arrival airport & transfer math

All international layovers arrive at **[Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport (CAN)](/airports/can/)**, roughly 28 km north of central Guangzhou:

- **Metro Line 3 (best choice):** direct from the airport into Tianhe and central Guangzhou in **45–55 minutes** for about **¥8**. It runs until roughly **22:30–23:00** and avoids highway traffic.
- **Taxi or DiDi:** **45–60 minutes** and **¥100–130**, worthwhile if you are traveling with a group, heavy bags, or landing late at night after the metro closes. Set up [DiDi ride-hailing](/guides/didi-and-ride-hailing-in-china/) before departure so addresses translate cleanly.
- **Immigration timing:** CAN handles heavy overnight waves from Europe, Australia, and Southeast Asia. Budget 60–90 minutes at peak arrival banks and return at least **2.5 hours** before an international departure.

## Planning by layover length

- **Under 6 hours:** stay airside. Once immigration and the 2.5-hour return buffer are counted, heading into town is not worth the stress.
- **6–8 hours:** ride Metro Line 3 straight to a morning dim sum meal, then walk **Shamian Island** (leafy riverside lanes and historic architecture) before heading back.
- **8–10 hours:** add **Canton Tower** or a street-food loop along **Beijing Road** after dim sum and Shamian Island. See our full [Guangzhou 6, 8, 10, 12, and 24-hour layover itinerary](/guides/guangzhou-layover-itinerary/) for exact neighborhood sequencing and dim sum timing.
- **12–24 hours:** add **Chen Clan Ancestral Hall**, a Pearl River night cruise, and a proper Cantonese roast-meat dinner (or a 30-minute metro side trip to Foshan).

## Where to stay: China Southern free hotel vs. booking downtown

Guangzhou is the flagship hub for **China Southern's free transit accommodation**, and unlike China Eastern, standard economy tickets usually qualify on 6–30 hour overnight connections (or same-day arrivals between 00:00 and 08:00 with 6+ hours). Book online before flying and confirm at **Gate 50 on the first floor of Terminal 2** upon arrival. Read the full terms, single-room supplement rules, and domestic-leg exclusions in our [China airline free transit hotel guide](/guides/china-airline-layover-hotel/).

If you do not qualify or prefer to pick your own neighborhood:

- **Short overnight (<12 hours):** stay near Baiyun Airport with a free terminal shuttle so you maximize sleep.
- **16–24 hour layover:** stay in **Tianhe** (direct on Metro Line 3, fast airport return) or **Liwan / Yuexiu** (best for waking up next to classic dim sum houses and Shamian Island). Hotels automatically complete mandatory [foreigner accommodation registration](/guides/hotel-registration-for-foreigners-in-china/).

## Practical logistics on the ground

- **Entry rules:** CAN is a designated port under the [240-hour visa-free transit policy](/transit-visa/), covering Guangdong province (and cross-province permitted areas). Confirm whether you use 240-hour transit or 30-day visa-free entry in [can you leave the airport during a China layover?](/guides/can-you-leave-the-airport-during-a-china-layover/).
- **Luggage:** Baiyun Airport offers staffed left-luggage counters (~¥15–30 per bag per day). Check options in [luggage storage at China's airports](/guides/luggage-storage-at-china-airports/).
- **Payments and connectivity:** small Cantonese eateries and tea houses are almost entirely QR-based. Link a foreign card to both Alipay and [WeChat Pay](/guides/wechat-pay-and-tenpaygo-for-foreigners/) before flying, install a travel eSIM ([best eSIM for China](/guides/best-esim-for-china/)), and keep passport-login [China airport Wi-Fi](/guides/china-airport-wifi/) as a backup.

## Food and local traps

Prioritize **dim sum** (*har gow*, *siu mai*, *char siu bao*, *cheung fun*), **Cantonese roast goose**, **wonton noodles**, and **double-skin milk dessert**. Two common mistakes to avoid: trying to chain distant districts (Canton Tower, old town Liwan, and Baiyun) inside a short 6–7 hour window, and landing at 3pm expecting morning dim sum carts when most traditional tea houses have already wound down.
