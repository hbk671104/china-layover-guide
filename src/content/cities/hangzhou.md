## Why Hangzhou works for a layover

Hangzhou is the classic Chinese postcard: a vast lake ringed by pagodas and willow trees, temples tucked into forested hills, and tea fields on the edge of the city. It is also only about an hour from Shanghai by high-speed rail, so it works as a day trip from a Shanghai stop as well as a layover on its own.

## The route

Start at **West Lake**, walk the **Su Causeway** or the shoreline near the broken bridge. Head to **Lingyin Temple**, one of China's most important Buddhist temples, set among carvings and forest. Then visit the **Longjing (Dragon Well) tea village** for the tea terraces and a tasting. If time allows, **Hefang Street** and the illuminated **Leifeng Pagoda** round out the day.

## Food to try

Longjing shrimp (cooked with the local green tea), West Lake fish in vinegar, and Dongpo pork. Hangzhou cuisine is lighter and sweeter than Sichuan or Cantonese food.

## Timing notes

Hangzhou is compact, so a well-planned 8–10 hours is genuinely rewarding. If you are coming from Shanghai by high-speed rail, remember it departs from Hongqiao station, not the airport, and you need your passport to book and board.

## Pairing with Shanghai

A common route is to land in Shanghai, ride the high-speed train to Hangzhou for West Lake, and return.
