## The short answer

Chinese airports do offer free Wi-Fi, and at the four biggest hubs, **PEK, PKX, PVG and CAN, a foreign passport is all you need to log in. No Chinese mobile number required.** SMS verification still exists, but it is one option among several, and for foreign travelers the passport route is the recommended one.

## How each hub signs you in

Chinese public networks require real-name registration, which is why the login is gated. The passport route now covers the main arrival airports:

| Airport | How to log in with a passport |
|---|---|
| Beijing Capital (PEK) | Passport-scan kiosk prints a username and password, about 5 hours per code, up to 3 print-outs per ID |
| Beijing Daxing (PKX) | Photograph your passport page on the authorization page (SSID **BDIA-FREE-WIFI**) |
| Shanghai Pudong (PVG) | Choose **Passport Login** on the portal page, about 4 hours per code |
| Guangzhou Baiyun (CAN) | Self-service terminal reads your passport or boarding pass, about 5 hours per code |

Look for the kiosk or terminal near the arrivals exits, or for the passport option on the portal page once you join the SSID. Each code covers a limited window, so on a long connection expect to log in again.

## What actually works

| Option | Works on arrival? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Passport login at PEK, PKX, PVG, CAN** | ✅ Yes | No Chinese number needed |
| **Travel eSIM activated before landing** | ✅ Yes | Works even where the passport route does not |
| Airport Wi-Fi with an SMS code | ⚠️ Sometimes | Only if a roaming SMS reaches you |
| Local SIM bought at the airport | ✅ After purchase | Needs a counter visit and passport registration |
| Roaming on your home SIM | ✅ Yes | Expensive but works |

## The five-minute fix

Before you fly:

1. **Buy a travel eSIM** and install it on home Wi-Fi, see [Best eSIM for China](/guides/best-esim-for-china/).
2. **Test it** is not possible until you land, so instead confirm the install completed and the plan shows as active.
3. **Enable it** just before landing.
4. **Open a map** in the terminal to confirm data is flowing.

That way you land with data even if the airport login fails for any reason.

## If the login fails

1. Look for the airport's **passport-scan kiosk** or a staffed information desk.
2. Ask airport staff, many terminals have a help desk that can issue a code.
3. Use a **coffee shop or lounge** network; some are less strictly gated.
4. As a last resort, **buy a local SIM** at a carrier counter (bring your passport).

## Bottom line

Airport Wi-Fi in China is no longer a trap for foreign travelers: at PEK, PKX, PVG and CAN your passport gets you a code, and an SMS is just one of several options.
