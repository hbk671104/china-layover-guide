## The short answer

**Ten hours is the first window where Guangzhou feels unhurried.** You get roughly **5–6 usable hours**, which is enough for dim sum, Shamian Island, Canton Tower, and a street-food walk — provided you eat at the right hour.

## The hour-by-hour plan

Assuming a mid-morning landing. Shift the whole plan if you land later, and skip to the "if you land in the afternoon" note below.

| Clock | Step |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Land at CAN, clear immigration (allow 45–90 min) |
| 1:15 | Store bags, or leave them in the car if you hired one |
| 1:45 | **Metro Line 3** into the city (~45–55 min, ~¥8) |
| 2:45 | **Dim sum** — the main event. Allow 1–1.5 hours |
| 4:15 | **Shamian Island** — a slow hour among the colonial-era buildings |
| 5:30 | Metro to **Canton Tower** / Huacheng Square |
| 6:00 | Skyline photos; go up the tower only if it is clear |
| 7:15 | **Beijing Road** or Shangxiajiu for street food and a walk |
| 8:15 | Metro Line 3 back to CAN (45–55 min) |
| 9:15 | At the airport — 2.5-hour buffer before departure |

That lands you at the gate with time to spare and no sprinting.

## The order matters — eat first

This is the single biggest difference between a good Guangzhou layover and a wasted one. **Dim sum is a morning and lunch ritual.** Locals arrive early; the good teahouses fill by noon and the kitchens wind down through the afternoon.

So the sequence is not negotiable if you land before about 2pm: **airport → dim sum → everything else.**

### If you land in the afternoon

Flip it. Do Shamian Island and Canton Tower first, then eat Cantonese **roast goose or roast meats** for dinner instead. You will not get proper yum cha, but you will get excellent Cantonese food — and that is still the point of Guangzhou.

## What to order

- **Har gow** — shrimp dumplings, the benchmark
- **Siu mai** — pork and shrimp dumplings
- **Char siu bao** — barbecue pork buns
- **Cheung fun** — silky rice noodle rolls
- **Roast goose** — Guangzhou's signature dish, and best here

Order a pot of tea and let it come in waves. Dim sum is a slow meal by design.

## Getting around

- **Metro Line 3** is your spine — 45–55 minutes to the airport, ~¥8, traffic-proof.
- **Between the highlights**, the metro is usually faster than a taxi in Guangzhou traffic.
- **Canton Tower and Shamian Island are in different directions**, so order them by geography, not preference.

## The two things that will cost you the flight

1. **Immigration queues.** CAN is a major hub for Africa, Southeast Asia, and Europe traffic. Allow a real 60–90 minutes.
2. **The last metro.** Line 3 stops running around 22:30–23:00. If your buffer runs past that, your return becomes a taxi or DiDi and you should leave the city 30 minutes earlier.

## Before you land

- **Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay** — teahouses and street stalls are QR-only. See [Alipay for foreigners](/guides/alipay-for-foreigners/).
- **Install an eSIM** so maps and translation work on arrival. See [eSIM setup](/guides/esim-setup-before-you-land/).
- **Confirm you can enter** — check [can I leave CAN on a layover?](/guides/can-i-leave-guangzhou-baiyun-airport-on-a-layover/).

## Bottom line

Ten hours in Guangzhou is a real day out if you eat first and keep to the metro. Dim sum, Shamian Island, the Canton Tower skyline, and street food — in that order.
