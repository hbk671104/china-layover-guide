## The short answer

**DiDi (滴滴出行) is China's Uber**, and it is the easiest way for a foreigner to get around. It has an English interface, accepts foreign phone numbers, and can be paid with an international card or through Alipay. Set it up **before you fly**, because SMS verification and app stores are unreliable once you are in China.

## Set it up

1. **Install the right app.** Search for **"DiDi Chuxing" (滴滴出行)** — the domestic China app. The separate international "DiDi Rider" app does **not** work in mainland China.
2. **Switch to English** in *Settings → Language*.
3. **Register with your phone number** and enter the SMS code. Do this at home on your own network.
4. **Add a payment method** under *Me → Wallet → Payment Methods*. Add a card, and enable auto-deduct.
5. **If the card fails**, link it in Alipay instead and choose Alipay as your DiDi payment method — that path is usually more reliable.

## Booking a ride

1. Check the **pickup pin** — drag it to where you are actually standing, not across the road.
2. Enter your destination. English names work for major hotels and landmarks; pasting a **Chinese address** is more reliable for smaller places.
3. Choose a ride type:

| Type | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Express (快车)** | Cheapest, fine for almost every trip |
| **Premier (专车)** | Nicer car, good for airports |
| **Taxi (出租车)** | Hails a metered taxi through the app |
| **Express Pool (拼车)** | Shared, cheaper, slower |

4. Confirm and wait — typically 2–5 minutes in city centers.
5. **Before getting in: check the plate number matches the app.** If it does not, cancel.

## The "last four digits" ritual

Drivers commonly ask for the **last four digits of your registered phone number** as an identity check. Write them down or keep them handy in a note — easier than saying numbers in a language you may not speak.

## Prices to expect

| Trip | Approximate fare |
|---|---|
| Short central trip (3–5 km) | ¥15–30 |
| Cross-city (15+ km) | ¥50–100 |
| Airport to city (Shanghai) | ¥150–250 |

Fares are shown before you confirm, and surge pricing applies at rush hour, in bad weather, and on holidays.

## If you'd rather not install another app

**DiDi runs inside Alipay and WeChat** as a mini-program. Open Alipay, tap the **Transport** area, and choose Taxi/Ride-hailing. You get the same driver pool with one less app — and Alipay's foreign-card handling is often smoother. See the [China apps checklist](/guides/china-apps-checklist/).

## Staying safe

- **Match the plate** before boarding, every time.
- **Use Share Trip** to send your route to someone.
- **Ignore touts** at airport exits offering rides — unlicensed and overpriced.
- **Use the official pickup zone** (follow signs for *Online Car-hailing / 网约车*).
- **Lost an item?** Use the in-app lost-item feature and offer a small reward.

## Bottom line

DiDi is the single most useful transport app for a China layover. Install it at home, link a card (or Alipay), check the plate, and you can move around a Chinese city without speaking a word of Mandarin.
