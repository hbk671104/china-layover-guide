## The short answer

**China is a very safe country for tourists.** Violent crime against visitors is rare and city streets are safe at night. The risks you actually face are petty and predictable: **taxi overcharging, tourist scams near major sights, and pickpocketing in crowds.** Handle those and you will be fine.

## The real risks, ranked

### 1. Unlicensed taxis at airports
Touts approach you in arrivals offering a "taxi". They overcharge heavily.

**Avoid it:** use the **official taxi queue** or book **DiDi**. Never follow someone who approaches you. See [DiDi and ride-hailing](/guides/didi-and-ride-hailing-in-china/).

### 2. The "tea ceremony" and "art student" scams
Near major tourist sights, friendly English-speaking strangers invite you to a tea ceremony, an art exhibition, or a student practice session. The bill at the end is enormous.

**Avoid it:** decline unsolicited invitations from strangers, however friendly. Real invitations do not come from someone who approached you on the street.

### 3. Aggressive touts
Souvenir sellers, "massage" touts, and photo hustlers cluster around landmarks.

**Avoid it:** a firm "no thanks" and keep walking. Do not engage, do not follow.

### 4. Pickpocketing in crowds
Metro stations and night markets at peak hours.

**Avoid it:** front pockets or a zipped bag, and be aware when squeezed into crowds.

### 5. Overpriced "tour" offers
Door-to-door tour sellers offering Great Wall or city tours at inflated prices.

**Avoid it:** book through a reputable platform in advance, or organize it yourself.

## The genuine safety rules

- **Use DiDi or the official taxi queue.** Never an unmarked car.
- **Keep your passport secure** and carry a photocopy or photo separately.
- **Save your embassy details offline** before you travel.
- **Do not carry large amounts of cash**, see [do you need cash in China?](/guides/do-you-need-cash-in-china/).
- **Watch your step**, traffic, e-bikes, and scooters are the most likely way to actually get hurt.
- **Respect local rules** at attractions and on public transport.

## Health and practicalities

- **Tap water is not drinkable**, drink bottled or boiled water.
- **Pharmacies** are common; bring any prescription medication in original packaging with a copy of the prescription.
- **Travel insurance** is cheap and worth having for a longer stopover.
- **Air quality** in northern cities can be poor in winter; sensitive travelers should plan indoor stops.

## Bottom line

China is safer than most travelers expect. Your job is not to defend against violence. It is to avoid petty hustles by sticking to official channels: the taxi queue or DiDi, licensed attractions, and pre-booked transport.
