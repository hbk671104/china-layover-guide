## The short answer

Two different airlines sit behind this question, and they give different answers.

- **China Airlines** (the Taiwanese carrier, IATA code CI) does **not** publish a free transit hotel for an ordinary long connection. At Taipei it gives transit passengers a **free half-day tour** instead, and separately sells a **free stopover hotel** that you book as part of the itinerary.
- **Air China, China Eastern and China Southern** — the three mainland carriers — all run **free transit hotel** schemes for qualifying connections. They are genuinely free rather than discounted, but stricter than most travelers expect.

For the mainland three, the single most common reason people get refused is that their connection does not sit on **one through-ticket**. Separate tickets, even on the same airline, disqualify you.

Two other things catch people out:

1. **You must book before you fly.** None of the mainland three accept walk-up bookings at the airport counter on the day.
2. **You need entry permission.** Their hotels are landside, so you must clear immigration to reach them, which means using the [240-hour visa-free transit](/transit-visa/) or holding a visa.

## First, which "China Airlines" do you mean?

The two names are one word apart and constantly confused, so it is worth being sure which carrier is on your ticket.

| | China Airlines | Air China |
|---|---|---|
| Based in | Taiwan | Mainland China |
| IATA code | **CI** | **CA** |
| Main hub | Taipei Taoyuan (TPE) | Beijing Capital (PEK) and Daxing (PKX) |
| What a long layover gets you | A free half-day tour | A free transit hotel |

If you are flying **into mainland China**, your answer is the Air China, China Eastern or China Southern rules below. If you are **transiting Taipei**, read the China Airlines section next — and note that mainland China's 240-hour visa-free transit does not apply to a Taipei transit, because Taiwan runs its own entry rules.

## China Airlines (Taiwan): a free tour, not a free hotel

**What you get on a long layover:** a free half-day tour of Taipei or northern Taiwan.

- **Who qualifies:** transit and transfer passengers with a **7–24 hour layover** before their onward flight, holding a valid R.O.C. visa or travelling on a passport eligible for Taiwan visa-exempt entry.
- **How to book:** online pre-registration, from **10 to 45 days before arrival**. There are 18 seats, so early booking matters.
- **Departure times:** morning tour from 08:15–08:30, back by 12:30; afternoon from 13:45–14:00, back by 18:00; evening from 18:10, back by 22:30. Pickup is at the Terminal 1 or Terminal 2 Tourist Service Centre.
- **The tours:** a Taipei landmarks circuit, a traditional-arts and nature run to Daxi, or a night-market food tour.

Registration runs through the [Taiwan Tourism Administration](https://halfdaytour.taiwan.net.tw/) and the terms are on [China Airlines' own tour page](https://flights.china-airlines.com/en-tw/twn_halfday_tour).

**So where is the hotel?** China Airlines publishes a Taipei **stopover** hotel programme, "Discover Taiwan", on its own site. Read that as a stopover product: you build the stopover into the itinerary when you book, rather than collecting a hotel because your connection happens to be long. Terms are on the [China Airlines stopover page](https://taipeistopover.service.china-airlines.com/). Confirm them with the airline before you book — unlike the mainland transit hotels below, stopover promotions have come and gone over the years.

**Do not assume the mainland rules transfer.** If your itinerary touches both Taiwan and the mainland, check each side separately. Taiwan's visa-exempt entry and mainland China's [240-hour visa-free transit](/transit-visa/) are unrelated schemes with different country lists.

## China Eastern: the free transit hotel

China Eastern calls this the **Overnight Transit Hotel Service**, and you will also see it filed as **STPC**, Stopover Paid by Carrier, the industry term for an airline covering your hotel because the connection is long. China Eastern's own page keywords describe it as "free transit hotels".

**What you need:**

- A ticket number beginning **781**, China Eastern's own ticket stock, on flights operated by China Eastern (MU) or Shanghai Airlines (FM)
- A transit through **Shanghai, Beijing, Kunming, Nanjing or Wuxi**
- A transit time of **6–24 hours**, and your onward flight must be the earliest available departure to your destination
- A fare at or above the minimum for your hub and transfer type, reported around **RMB 1,800** at Shanghai, and lower at Kunming (RMB 400–600)
- Booking classes **A, O, X** and domestic **H** are excluded
- Advance booking, with a cutoff reported at **12 hours before your first flight's departure**

**The same-day rule is the one that surprises people.** Free accommodation on a same-day transit is only offered if your inbound flight arrives **before 03:00**. A 05:10 arrival connecting to a 13:55 departure the same day does *not* qualify.

One honest warning: China Eastern's terms are the least readable of the three. The application page loads its conditions behind a script that redirects ordinary browsers, and travelers report the online form failing and phone agents redirecting them back to the site. You must also be **logged into the China Eastern mileage programme** for the application option to appear at all. Call **+86-21-95530** if the form won't cooperate.

## China Southern: the free transit hotel

China Southern publishes clearer rules than China Eastern, and its scheme is the one most travelers actually manage to use. It covers Guangzhou, Beijing, Shenzhen, Urumqi, Shenyang, Dalian, Harbin, Wuhan, Changsha and Zhengzhou, with online booking available for Guangzhou, Beijing, Urumqi, Shenzhen and Shenyang.

**Two transit windows apply:**

- **Overnight connections:** 6–30 hours for domestic↔international and international↔international
- **Same-day connections:** the first flight must arrive between **00:00 and 08:00**, and you need at least 6 hours
- Pure **international↔international** connections can run up to **240 hours**, but you get the **first night only**

**Economy passengers usually do qualify.** This is the key difference from China Eastern. Standard economy gets a **C-grade hotel**, assigned as **one room for two people** of the same sex; you can pay a supplement to upgrade to a single room. Business and first class get A-grade; elite frequent flyers get better grades too.

The counter-example worth knowing: a Guangzhou–Beijing domestic connection after an international flight does **not** qualify, even on one ticket. If the onward leg is domestic, check before you count on the hotel.

**To claim it:** book through the China Southern website (Home → Service Center → Transit Hotel Booking), the app, or the WeChat mini program. At Guangzhou, the transit accommodation counter is at **Gate 50 on the first floor of Terminal 2**, near the domestic arrival exit: staff there confirm your booking and arrange the free shuttle. If you didn't book online, you can still apply at the counter, subject to availability.

## Air China: the free transit hotel

Air China's product is called **Transit Accommodation**, and it splits into two tiers:

- **Deluxe:** Beijing (Capital), Chengdu (Tianfu), Shanghai (Pudong), bookable only via the sales hotline **95583**
- **Standard:** the above plus Beijing (Daxing), Hangzhou, Tianjin, Wenzhou, bookable online, in the app, or by phone

**What you need:**

- Ticket number starting **999**
- Flight numbers carrying the **CA code**, with qualifying operating carriers (for international↔international, both legs must be operated by Air China itself)
- **6–24 hours** for domestic-to-domestic; **6–30 hours** when an international segment is involved
- Same-day transits need the inbound flight to land before **08:00**
- Booking deadline: **24 hours** before the inbound flight's arrival for Standard, **50 hours** for Deluxe, Beijing time
- Excluded: booking classes **O, I, X, N**, mileage and award tickets, and infants

Air China states plainly that **on-site bookings are not accepted**, and that partner hotels have limited rooms, qualifying does not guarantee you one. It also notes that where a shuttle exists it may only serve one airport, so a PEK-to-PKX or PVG-to-SHA connection can leave you arranging and paying for your own transfer.

## Other mainland carriers with free transit hotels

The three majors are not the only carriers that do this, and on some of the others the window is far more generous.

| Carrier | Where | Transit window | The condition that catches people |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hainan Airlines** (HU) | Haikou (HAK), Chongqing (CKG) | Over 4 and up to 72 hours at Haikou; over 6 and up to 48 hours at Chongqing | The ticket has to be **bought in the Hainan Airlines app**. At Chongqing the free hotel and the free tour are **either/or**. Beijing offers a hotel discount, not a free night. |
| **Xiamen Airlines** (MF) | Xiamen, Fuzhou, Quanzhou, Hangzhou | 6–24 hours, **and** the two flights must not depart on the same calendar day | Both segments on **one ticket** operated by Xiamen, Hebei or Jiangxi Airlines, and the international leg must be Xiamen-operated. Award and privilege-redemption classes (X, O, E) are excluded. |
| **Sichuan Airlines** (3U) | Chengdu Shuangliu (CTU) and Tianfu (TFU) | 6–48 hours domestic; **6–240 hours** if any leg is international | Ticket number starting **876**. Book from 30 days out until 4 hours before the onward flight departs. |
| **Shenzhen Airlines** (ZH) | Shenzhen Bao'an (SZX) only | 6–36 hours with an international leg; 6–24 hours domestic | Both segments must be operated by Shenzhen Airlines. |

A few details worth knowing:

- **Sichuan Airlines has the widest window** — up to 240 hours if your itinerary touches an international leg — and it states plainly that the hotel was never bundled into your fare and you pay nothing for it. If you are connecting through Chengdu, see the [TFU layover guide](/airports/tfu/). Chengdu's two airports are both covered, and a dual-airport transit can pick a hotel at either.
- **Hainan's free night is an airport hotel**: one standard room for two people for one night, with a ¥200 supplement for single occupancy, subject to availability in peak season.
- **Xiamen's hotel is landside.** International-to-international passengers have to complete temporary entry formalities at the airport before they can reach it, so you cannot stay airside. Xiamen's English-language page still carries a 2023 expiry date; the live rule took effect on 10 January 2025.
- **A single room usually costs extra.** Xiamen assigns economy passengers two to a standard room, with a supplement of around ¥175–180 for single occupancy. China Southern works the same way.

**And the carriers that do not:** Juneyao and Spring Airlines publish no free transit hotel of their own. Spring's page lists free meals and rooms at several airports, but they are **airport** schemes rather than airline products, each with its own booking channel and deadline — useful if you are flying Spring, but not something the airline will arrange for you.

## If you don't qualify

Plenty of travelers won't. The usual reasons are a separate ticket, a fare below the threshold, or an onward leg that's domestic.

**When the airline says no, you have three options:**

1. **Book an airport hotel yourself in advance.** Several Chinese airports have hotels in or beside the terminal that sell short blocks of 4–6 hours: often cheaper and better located than a full-night booking. See [luggage storage at China's airports](/guides/luggage-storage-at-china-airports/) if you'd rather not drag bags.
2. **Stay in the city instead.** If you qualify for [240-hour visa-free transit](/transit-visa/), you can leave the airport entirely. Check that first. It may be a better night than an airport hotel. Note that [foreign visitors must register their accommodation](/guides/hotel-registration-for-foreigners-in-china/), which hotels handle automatically at check-in.
3. **Use a lounge.** Cheaper than a room and airside, so no immigration needed.

If your layover is short enough that a hotel is overkill but long enough to be tedious, see whether it's worth [leaving the airport at all](/guides/can-you-leave-the-airport-during-a-china-layover/), and how much time you actually need.

## Booking it: the checklist

1. **Confirm the ticket is one through-ticket** under a single booking reference, with the right ticket-number prefix for your carrier.
2. **Check your booking class** against the exclusions. Cheap promotional fares are the ones most often excluded.
3. **Measure the transit window** against the correct rule for your connection type, overnight and same-day have different thresholds, and some carriers have a separate, longer window for international↔international.
4. **Book through the airline's own channel** well before the deadline.
5. **Save your confirmation offline.** Screenshot the hotel voucher and booking reference before you fly, airport Wi-Fi is not something to rely on at the moment you need it, and the coverage situation is covered in [China airport Wi-Fi](/guides/china-airport-wifi/).
6. **Confirm your entry permission.** The hotel is landside, so sort out the [240-hour visa-free transit](/transit-visa/) or a visa before you travel.

## Bottom line

Check which airline you actually booked. **China Airlines** (Taiwan, CI) gives a free half-day tour on a 7–24 hour Taipei layover, not a free hotel. The three mainland carriers — **Air China, China Eastern and China Southern** — do give a free transit hotel, and China Southern is the most generous of the majors in practice because ordinary economy fares qualify. Smaller carriers can be more generous still: Sichuan Airlines covers connections of up to 240 hours out of Chengdu.

The catch is never the hotel. It's the paperwork: one through-ticket, a qualifying booking class, the right transit window, and a booking made before you fly rather than at the counter.

Book it at the same time as your flights, not the week before. Rooms are capped at every carrier, and "eligible" and "guaranteed" are not the same word.
