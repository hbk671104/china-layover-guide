## The short answer

China's visa-free transit is for **passing through**, not for a round trip. Your journey must be **A → China → C**, where **C is different from A**, and you must hold a **confirmed onward ticket** to that third country or region. If you plan to fly home the way you came, the rule does not apply.

## The A→B→C rule

| Leg | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | The country or region you depart from |
| **B** | China, where you transit |
| **C** | A **different** country or region you travel on to |

The point is that you are genuinely in transit. The onward destination must not be the place you arrived from.

## What qualifies, and what does not

| Route | Qualifies? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| London → Shanghai → Tokyo | Yes | C differs from A |
| New York → Beijing → Bangkok | Yes | C differs from A |
| London → Shanghai → Hong Kong | Generally yes | Hong Kong is a separate region |
| London → Shanghai → London | **No** | Round trip — C equals A |
| New York → Beijing → Chicago | **No** | Return to the same country |
| Paris → Beijing → Shanghai → Tokyo | Usually yes, with care | Domestic legs are allowed within the permitted areas; your onward international ticket must be to a third country |

## Do Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan count?

**Generally yes, each as a separate region.** Routes like Shanghai → Hong Kong or Beijing → Macau are commonly used to satisfy the onward-leg requirement, and Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan each count independently. Because this is decided by border officers and airline check-in staff, **confirm your exact itinerary with your airline and the NIA** before you rely on it.

## Separate tickets are fine

You do not need one single booking. A separate onward ticket works, but you must be able to **show it** — a booking confirmation with a date and flight number. Keep a screenshot on your phone, since airport Wi-Fi may need an SMS code you cannot receive. Airlines check carefully because they are penalized if they board an ineligible passenger.

## The related requirement: permitted areas

Satisfying A→B→C is necessary but not sufficient. You must also:

- Enter and exit through a **designated port**.
- Stay within the **permitted areas** for the participating provinces.
- Depart within the **allowed time** (240 hours for the transit policy).

See the [ports and permitted areas reference](/guides/240-hour-transit-ports-and-permitted-areas/) for the details.

## Bottom line

A→B→C is the rule that trips up the most travelers: it is not enough to have a long stop, your onward ticket must go to a **different** country or region. If you are returning the same way you came, use [30-day visa-free entry](/guides/30-day-visa-free-vs-240-hour-transit/) if you qualify, or apply for a visa.
