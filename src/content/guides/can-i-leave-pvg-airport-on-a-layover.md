## The short answer

**Yes — PVG is a designated port, and most eligible travelers can leave the airport.** Whether it is *worth* it depends on your layover length: **7 hours is the practical minimum, 8+ is comfortable.** Under 6 hours, stay airside.

## Can you enter?

PVG is a designated port under China's **240-hour visa-free transit** policy, so eligible travelers can enter and travel within the **Shanghai municipality**.

You need:

1. A passport from one of the **55 eligible countries**.
2. A **confirmed onward ticket to a third country or region** departing within 240 hours. Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan count as separate regions.
3. Entry at a designated port — PVG qualifies.
4. A passport valid at least **3 months** beyond entry.

> Not on the transit list? Many nationalities qualify for China's separate **30-day visa-free entry**, which has no third-country requirement and is simpler. See [30-day visa-free entry vs 240-hour transit](/guides/30-day-visa-free-vs-240-hour-transit/).

## You already have a Chinese visa?

Then the transit rules do not matter — use your visa. The visa-free schemes only apply when you do not hold one.

## How much time you need

| Layover | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Under 5 hours | **Stay airside.** Immigration alone can take 90 minutes. |
| 5–6 hours | **Rushed.** You would reach the city and turn around. |
| 7–8 hours | **Worth it.** The Bund and a meal. |
| 10–12 hours | **Comfortable.** A real half-day. |
| 12+ hours | **A proper stopover.** |

The PVG-specific constraint: **immigration queues here are among the worst of China's big gateways.** An evening bank of long-haul arrivals can mean 60–90 minutes in the transit lane. Build that in.

## The way out

1. **At check-in**, mention you are using visa-free transit — airlines check your onward ticket before boarding.
2. **Fill in the Arrival Card** on the plane.
3. **Join the 24/240-hour visa-free transit lane** at immigration (not the regular foreigner queue).
4. **Store your bags** if you are not checking into a hotel — left-luggage is usually ¥15–30 per bag.
5. **Take the Maglev plus Metro Line 2** into the centre.

## The traps that catch people

- **PVG and SHA are 70 km apart.** If your connection uses both airports, that is a 1.5–2 hour transfer, and you should not add sightseeing under 12 hours.
- **The Maglev stops around 21:40.** Late returns become taxi or DiDi.
- **A round trip does not qualify.** London → Shanghai → London fails the third-country rule; London → Shanghai → Tokyo works.
- **The transit lane is slower than the regular queue,** because officers manually check your itinerary.

## Bottom line

PVG is a genuine gateway — you can leave, and with 8 hours you should. Check your eligibility first, take the Maglev, and be back 2.5 hours before departure. For the itinerary itself, see [PVG 8-hour layover](/guides/pvg-8-hour-layover/) and the [PVG airport guide](/airports/pvg/).
