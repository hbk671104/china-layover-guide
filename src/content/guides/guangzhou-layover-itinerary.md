## The short answer

Guangzhou is China's food capital and one of its easier layovers: the airport connects by metro in under an hour, and the city rewards eating over sightseeing. With **6 hours you can do one great dim sum meal**; with **8 hours you add Shamian Island**; with **10 hours** you get the full classic day; with **12 hours** you add Canton Tower; with **24 hours** you eat your way through and add a day trip.

The single most important adjustment: **eat early.** Yum cha is a morning and lunch ritual, and landing in the afternoon misses it.

## Airport to city

| Option | Time | Cost | Traffic-proof | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Metro Line 3** | 45–55 min | ~¥8 | Yes | Almost everyone |
| **Taxi** | 45–60 min | ¥100–130 | No | Heavy bags, groups |
| **DiDi** | 45–60 min | ¥100–130 | No | No Mandarin needed |
| **Airport bus** | 60–90 min | ¥15–30 | No | Specific hotels or stations |

Baiyun Airport (CAN) is well connected and the metro is straightforward.

**Metro Line 3, step by step:**

1. Follow the **Metro / 地铁** signs from arrivals down to the station.
2. Buy a single ticket from the machines (they take cash and QR payments), or scan a **transport QR code** in Alipay or WeChat Pay.
3. Board **Line 3** toward the city.
4. Get off at your destination: Tianhe, Guangzhou East, or a central interchange.

**Take a car instead when** you have heavy or multiple bags, you are a group of three or four, you land late (the metro stops around 22:30–23:00), or you want to save the interchange walk on a tight layover. [DiDi](/guides/didi-and-ride-hailing-in-china/) is the easiest car option: English interface, fares shown upfront, built-in chat translation.

**Paying:** buy metro tickets with cash or an Alipay/WeChat Pay transport QR. Taxis are metered and take cash or QR, but most drivers do not accept foreign cards. If you have not set up a payment app, do it before you fly: see [how to pay in China](/guides/payments-in-china-for-foreigners/).

**Rules that save you trouble:** use the official taxi queue, have your destination in Chinese characters on your phone, check the last metro time if you land late, and never take an unlicensed "black car".

## What each window gives you

### 6 hours: one meal, done well
Immigration plus the transfer plus the return buffer leaves roughly **1.5–2 hours in the city**. That is enough for a memorable dim sum meal and not enough for a second stop.

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Land at CAN |
| 0:45–1:15 | Clear immigration |
| 2:00 | Metro Line 3 into the city |
| 2:00–3:30 | **Dim sum lunch** |
| 3:30 | Head back |
| 5:00 | At the airport, 2.5-hour buffer |

### 8 hours: dim sum and Shamian Island
About **3–4 usable hours**. Take Metro Line 3 in, eat a proper **dim sum breakfast or lunch** (arrive before 11am when locals go), then walk **Shamian Island**, leafy colonial-era streets perfect for a slow hour.

### 10 hours: the classic day, hour by hour
Assuming a mid-morning landing. Shift the whole plan if you land later.

| Clock | Step |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Land at CAN, clear immigration (allow 45–90 min) |
| 1:15 | Store bags, or leave them in the car if you hired one |
| 1:45 | **Metro Line 3** into the city (~45–55 min, ~¥8) |
| 2:45 | **Dim sum**: the main event. Allow 1–1.5 hours |
| 4:15 | **Shamian Island**: a slow hour among the colonial-era buildings |
| 5:30 | Metro to **Canton Tower** / Huacheng Square |
| 6:00 | Skyline photos; go up the tower only if it is clear |
| 7:15 | **Beijing Road** or Shangxiajiu for street food and a walk |
| 8:15 | Metro Line 3 back to CAN (45–55 min) |
| 9:15 | At the airport, 2.5-hour buffer before departure |

That is about **5–6 usable hours**, and it lands you at the gate with time to spare. If you have time in hand, **Chen Clan Ancestral Hall** is worth slotting in for the architecture.

### 12 hours: add Canton Tower and a night cruise
About **6 usable hours** plus a comfortable margin. Do the 10-hour plan and add a **Pearl River night cruise** if your return buffer allows, or finish at **Beijing Road** for street food and shopping.

### 24 hours: eat your way through
Book a hotel downtown and treat it as a food trip: morning tea, roast goose for lunch, a proper Cantonese dinner. Add **Chen Clan Ancestral Hall** for the architecture, or a day trip to **Foshan** (about 30 minutes by metro).

## The order matters: eat first

This is the biggest difference between a good Guangzhou layover and a wasted one. **Dim sum is a morning and lunch ritual.** Locals arrive early; the good teahouses fill by noon and the kitchens wind down through the afternoon.

So if you land before about 2pm, the sequence is not negotiable: **airport, then dim sum, then everything else.**

**If you land in the afternoon**, flip it. Do Shamian Island and Canton Tower first, then eat Cantonese **roast goose or roast meats** for dinner instead. You will not get proper yum cha, but you will get excellent Cantonese food.

**Worth knowing before you plan:** Canton Tower and Shamian Island are in different directions, so order the highlights by geography, not preference. The observation deck costs extra and takes an hour; viewing the tower from Huacheng Square is free and nearly as impressive for photos. The illuminated **Pearl River cruises run after dark**, which usually conflicts with your return buffer, so a daytime river view from Shamian Island is the practical substitute.

## What to eat

Guangzhou is where Cantonese cooking comes from, so eat the classics:

- **Har gow** (shrimp dumplings), **siu mai**, **char siu bao**, **cheung fun** (rice noodle rolls)
- **Roast goose**, Guangzhou's signature, and better here than anywhere else
- **Wonton noodles** and **double-skin milk dessert**

Order a pot of tea and let it come in waves. Dim sum is a slow meal by design.

## Guangzhou layover tours: are they worth it?

**Worth it if you have 8+ hours and want zero logistics.** A private Guangzhou tour solves the two hard parts, the transfer and the dim sum timing, in a city where the highlights are spread out. **Not worth it under 6 hours**, and unnecessary if you are comfortable taking Metro Line 3 and eating on your own schedule.

A driver meets you at Baiyun arrivals with a name sign and tracks your flight. Typical itineraries run **4–12 hours** and cover a rotating set of highlights: Chen Clan Ancestral Hall, Shamian Island, Canton Tower, Huacheng Square, Xiguan old town, and usually dim sum at a local teahouse. Many operators tier them by layover length: a 3–4 hour express, a 5–6 hour combo, and a 7–8 hour immersion that adds the tower and a proper meal.

| Type | Indicative price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Small-group bus tour | from ~US$87 | Fixed departure, less flexible on timing |
| Private tour, airport pickup | ~US$158–205 per person | Varies by group size |
| "All inclusive" private | ~US$200–220 | Adds entrance fees and a dim sum meal |

**Most operators split pricing into "private tour only" and "all inclusive".** The cheaper tier usually excludes food and sometimes guide service; the higher tier adds entrance fees and one meal. Confirm which one you are buying. Treat all figures as indicative; they move with season and group size.

**Read the refund clause before you pay.** Several Guangzhou operators state explicitly that if immigration refuses your transit entry and you cannot join the tour, there is no refund, because the guide and driver were already reserved. Some list a "no refund on cancellation" policy outright. Your eligibility is your responsibility, not the operator's.

**Before you book:** confirm you can enter using the [eligibility checker](/transit-visa/), give accurate flight numbers so the operator can track you, check the minimum layover they require (many state 6–8 hours), ask what is excluded, and check whether solo travelers get a car or are put on metro and taxi.

**The middle path:** book a private transfer only (car and driver, no guide) and follow the plans above yourself, or take Metro Line 3 for roughly ¥8 each way.

## The two things that will cost you the flight

1. **Immigration queues.** CAN is a major hub for Africa, Southeast Asia, and Europe traffic. Allow a real 60–90 minutes.
2. **The last metro.** Line 3 stops running around 22:30–23:00. If your buffer runs past that, your return becomes a taxi or DiDi, and you should leave the city 30 minutes earlier.

Guangzhou is also spread out: the airport, the Tianhe business district, the old town, and Canton Tower are all some distance apart, so do not try to chain them on a short layover. And a round trip does not qualify for visa-free transit: Guangzhou → home is not a third country.

## Bottom line

Go to Guangzhou hungry and go early. Six hours buys one great meal; ten buys a full Cantonese day. The metro is the default way in, and a tour only pays off if you want the logistics handled.
