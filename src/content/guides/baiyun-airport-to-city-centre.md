## The short answer

**Take Metro Line 3.** About **45–55 minutes** for roughly **¥8**, direct into the Tianhe business district and the centre, and completely unaffected by traffic. Taxi and DiDi are comfortable with luggage but cost **¥100–130** and are at the mercy of Guangzhou traffic.

## The options compared

| Option | Time | Cost | Traffic-proof | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Metro Line 3** | 45–55 min | ~¥8 | Yes | Almost everyone |
| **Taxi** | 45–60 min | ¥100–130 | No | Heavy bags, groups |
| **DiDi** | 45–60 min | ¥100–130 | No | No Mandarin needed |
| **Airport bus** | 60–90 min | ¥15–30 | No | Specific hotels or stations |

## Metro Line 3, step by step

1. Follow the **Metro / 地铁** signs from arrivals down to the station.
2. Buy a single ticket from the machines (they take cash and QR payments), or scan a **transport QR code** in Alipay or WeChat Pay.
3. Board **Line 3** toward the city.
4. Get off at your destination — Tianhe, Guangzhou East, or a central interchange.

The metro is signed in English, cheap, and entirely manageable with luggage outside rush hour.

## When to take a car instead

- **Heavy or multiple bags.** Metro stations have stairs and busy carriages.
- **Late arrival.** The metro stops running around 22:30–23:00; check the last train before you rely on it.
- **A group of three or four.** A taxi split four ways costs about the same as four metro tickets and gets you door to door.
- **A tight layover.** A car removes the interchange walk and saves 10–20 minutes at each end.

**DiDi** is the easiest car option — an English interface, fares shown upfront, and built-in chat translation. See [DiDi and ride-hailing in China](/guides/didi-and-ride-hailing-in-china/).

## Paying

- **Metro:** buy a ticket with cash, or use an Alipay/WeChat Pay transport QR at the gates.
- **Taxi:** metered; pay with cash or a QR app. Most drivers do not accept foreign cards.
- **DiDi:** charged automatically to your linked card or Alipay.

If you have not set up a payment app, do it before you fly — see [Alipay for foreigners](/guides/alipay-for-foreigners/).

## Rules that save you trouble

- **Use the official taxi queue**, never a driver offering a ride inside arrivals.
- **Have your destination in Chinese characters** on your phone.
- **Check the last metro time** if your flight lands late.
- **Do not take an unlicensed "black car"** — the price is not better and the risk is real.

## Bottom line

The metro is the default for a Baiyun layover: fast, cheap, and predictable. Take a car only for luggage, groups, or late arrivals. For what to do once you arrive, see the [Guangzhou 6/10-hour guide](/guides/guangzhou-6-10-hour-layover/) and the [CAN airport page](/airports/can/).
