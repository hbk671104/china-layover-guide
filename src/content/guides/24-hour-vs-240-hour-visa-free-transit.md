## The short answer

The **24-hour rule** is for any nationality and covers a quick connection, including leaving the airport for a few hours. The **240-hour rule** is for citizens of 55 countries and grants up to **10 days**, but only with a confirmed onward ticket to a third country or region. If you have a long stop and an eligible passport, the 240-hour rule is the one you want.

## Side by side

| | 24-hour visa-free transit | 240-hour visa-free transit |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Almost all nationalities | Citizens of 55 countries |
| **How long** | Under 24 hours | Up to 240 hours (10 days) |
| **Onward ticket** | Needed onward travel | Confirmed ticket to a *third* country or region |
| **Clock starts** | From your scheduled arrival | 00:00 the day after entry |
| **Where** | Most international ports | 65 designated ports in 24 provinces |
| **Leave the airport?** | Yes, with a temporary entry permit | Yes, within the permitted areas |
| **Typical use** | A long connection | A genuine stopover or mini-trip |

## How the 24-hour rule works

Two different things get called "24-hour transit":

1. **Airside transit.** You stay in the international transit zone and change planes. No visa, no permit, any nationality. You cannot exit the airport.
2. **24-hour visa-free entry.** If you want to leave the airport, apply at the **24/240-hour visa-free transit counter** for a temporary entry permit. This is usually granted for a genuine short stay and requires your onward ticket.

Important: the 24-hour clock is counted from your **scheduled arrival time**, not from the next day. So a 24-hour connection is genuinely short — you cannot stretch it the way the 240-hour rule allows.

## How the 240-hour rule works

- Available to citizens of **55 countries**.
- You enter and exit through **65 designated ports** across **24 provinces**.
- The stay begins at **00:00 the day after entry**, effectively giving you 10 calendar days.
- You must hold a **confirmed onward ticket to a third country or region**, departing within 240 hours. Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan count as separate regions for this purpose.
- You can travel within the permitted areas, including **cross-province travel** between the participating provinces.

## Which one applies to you?

1. **Connecting in under 24 hours and not leaving the airport?** You need nothing — airside transit.
2. **Want to leave the airport for a few hours, any nationality?** Ask for the 24-hour entry permit at the transit counter.
3. **Eligible passport + a stop of a day or more + onward to a different country?** Use the 240-hour rule.
4. **Eligible passport, staying longer or arriving/departing in the same country?** Consider [30-day visa-free entry vs 240-hour transit](/guides/30-day-visa-free-vs-240-hour-transit/), or a regular visa.

## Bottom line

Both rules are about transit, not tourism for its own sake. If your stop is short, the 24-hour facility is universal and simple. If it's long and your passport qualifies, the 240-hour rule turns a layover into a trip — as long as your onward ticket goes to a third country.
