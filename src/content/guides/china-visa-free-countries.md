> This reference was last reviewed on 2026-09-24. Visa-free lists change in waves, and countries are added or lapse several times a year. Always confirm the current list for your nationality with the National Immigration Administration (NIA) or the 12367 hotline before booking.

## The short answer

"China visa-free" is not one list. It is several lists that overlap, and the one that applies to you depends on **why** you are entering China and **how long** you are staying:

| Scheme | Countries | Stay | Onward ticket needed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **240-hour visa-free transit** | 55 | 10 days | Yes, to a third country or region |
| **30-day visa-free entry** | ~50 | 30 days | No |
| **Hainan regional scheme** | 59+ | 30 days | No (Hainan only) |
| **Bilateral agreements** | ~29 | Usually 30 days | No |

The table below is the **240-hour visa-free transit list**, the scheme most relevant to a layover. For a round trip or a longer stay, the 30-day entry list is usually the better fit, and it is a different list.

## The 30-day visa-free entry list is a different list

China's unilateral visa-free entry policy lets citizens of around **50 countries** stay up to **30 days** for tourism, business, family visits, or transit, with **no onward-ticket requirement**. It covers most of the EU, the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, and several Gulf and Latin American countries, and it is renewed in waves (most countries through 31 December 2026).

Two things matter here:

- **It is broader and simpler than transit.** If your nationality is on this list, you can visit anywhere in mainland China and a round trip still qualifies. For most travelers it makes the 240-hour transit policy irrelevant.
- **The two lists differ.** The United States, for example, is on the 55-country transit list but not on the 30-day list. Check your nationality against both.

Because the 30-day list is revised frequently, this page does not reproduce it country by country. The authoritative list is the NIA's own, linked at the foot of this page.

## Hainan and bilateral schemes

Beyond the national rules, two more routes can get you in without a visa:

- **Hainan, 30 days.** Ordinary passport holders from **59 or more countries** can enter Hainan Province visa-free for tourism, business, family visits, medical treatment, conferences, and sports events. Hainan's list is broader than the national 30-day list, so some nationalities that do not qualify nationally still qualify for Hainan.
- **Bilateral agreements.** China has mutual visa-exemption agreements with around **29 countries**, including Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, the UAE, and Qatar. Where one exists it takes precedence over the general scheme and may be more generous.

## How to check whether you need a visa

1. **Find your nationality** on the 55-country transit list above.
2. **Check the 30-day entry list** on the NIA site. If you are on it, you almost certainly do not need a visa.
3. **Check for a bilateral agreement**, which is often the most flexible route.
4. **If your destination is Hainan**, check that regional scheme separately.
5. **Confirm with your airline** before booking, because the check-in agent applies the rules at boarding.

If none of the schemes covers you, see [Do you need a visa for China?](/guides/visa-free-vs-visa-china/) for the visa types, timelines, and costs.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the lists as interchangeable.** The transit list and the 30-day list overlap but are not identical.
- **Assuming a round trip qualifies for transit.** It does not. You must continue to a third country or region.
- **Entering at the wrong port.** The transit policy works only at the 65 designated ports.
- **Relying on a list you read months ago.** Countries are added and lapse throughout the year.
