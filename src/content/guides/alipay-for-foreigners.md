## The short answer

Alipay is the single most useful app to set up before a China trip. **Link your Visa or Mastercard, verify with your passport, and you can pay almost everywhere by QR code.** Do it at home: verification and SMS codes are far easier before you travel.

## Set it up, step by step

1. **Download Alipay** from your home app store before you fly.
2. **Register** with your own phone number and confirm the SMS code.
3. **Verify your identity**, open *Me → Settings → Account and Security → Identity Information*, choose *Foreigner → Passport*, upload a clear photo of your passport page, and complete the short face scan. Approval is usually quick, but can take up to 24 hours.
4. **Add your card**, go to *Me → Bank Cards → Add Card*, enter the number, expiry, and CVV, and approve your bank's 3-D Secure step. A small test charge appears and then reverses.
5. **Test a small payment** before you rely on it, buy a bottle of water at a convenience store.

## What it costs

| Amount | Typical fee |
|---|---|
| ¥200 or less | No platform fee |
| Over ¥200 | ~3% platform fee |
| Any amount, foreign-issued UnionPay card | Usually exempt from the platform fee |

That 3% is separate from your own bank's foreign-transaction fee. Card support covers Visa, Mastercard, JCB, Discover, and Diners Club; American Express works with some issuers but not universally. Foreign-issued **UnionPay** cards are the smoothest option if you have one.

## Limits worth knowing

Once verification clears, the regulatory ceiling is about **US$5,000 per transaction and US$50,000 per year**. Your app, bank, or card may apply lower limits. That is far more than a layover traveler will spend.

## The one failure mode to expect

Many street stalls print a **personal QR code**, which cannot accept foreign-linked cards. Merchants with a **business or aggregate code** work fine. If a scan fails:

- Ask the vendor for their merchant payment code.
- Try WeChat Pay instead.
- Fall back to cash, see [do you need cash in China?](/guides/do-you-need-cash-in-china/).

## Set up a backup

Carry a second method: either a second card in Alipay, WeChat Pay as a second app, or some RMB cash. One wallet is a single point of failure. See [WeChat Pay and TenPayGo for foreigners](/guides/wechat-pay-and-tenpaygo-for-foreigners/).

## Bottom line

Alipay with a linked foreign card is the default way to pay in China. Verify your passport and add your card before departure, test it once, and keep a backup. For the broader picture, see [payments in China for foreigners](/guides/payments-in-china-for-foreigners/).
