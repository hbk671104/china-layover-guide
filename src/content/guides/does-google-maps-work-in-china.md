## The short answer

**No, Google Maps is not reliable in mainland China.** Google services are blocked, and China's mapping data is often offset or incomplete anyway. Use **Amap (高德地图)** or **Baidu Maps** for navigation, and keep Google Maps only as something that might work over a travel eSIM.

## What's blocked and what isn't

| Service | On a local Chinese network | Over a travel eSIM |
|---|---|---|
| Google Maps | Blocked | Usually works |
| Gmail | Blocked | Usually works |
| WhatsApp | Restricted | Usually works |
| Instagram / Facebook / X | Blocked | Usually works |
| Amap / Baidu Maps | Works | Works |
| Alipay / WeChat Pay | Works | Works |
| Apple Maps | Partially works | Partially works |

Apple Maps is a partial exception. It is available on iPhones in China and uses local data, though detail and English quality vary.

## Why "it loads" does not mean "it works"

China's mapping coordinates are deliberately offset from global GPS standards. That means even when Google Maps renders, **the pin can sit in the wrong place** relative to the real street. This is a well-known cause of travelers being dropped at the wrong entrance or the wrong side of a river.

Use a Chinese map app and the problem disappears.

## The map apps that actually work

| App | Language | Best for | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amap** (Gaode, 高德地图) | English available | The default for foreign visitors: metro, bus and walking directions, and finding a place by its English name | Works everywhere in mainland China |
| **Baidu Maps** (百度地图) | Chinese-first | Finding a Chinese address, deepest local coverage | Works everywhere in mainland China |
| **Apple Maps** | Localised | iPhone users who want the built-in app | Partly works in China on local data; detail and English quality vary |
| **Google Maps** | English | Nothing useful while you are on a Chinese network | Only over a travel eSIM, and the coordinate offset still applies |

**Install Amap before you fly.** The reason is not convenience, it is that app-store behaviour inside China is unpredictable, and you do not want to be solving it on airport Wi-Fi. Both Amap and Baidu Maps are free.

**Amap (Gaode)**
- Best English support among the Chinese map apps.
- Reliable metro, bus, and walking directions.
- Good for finding a destination by its English name.

**Baidu Maps**
- Deepest coverage of Chinese addresses.
- Strong once you can paste or scan a Chinese address.

Both handle the coordinate-offset problem natively, which is the part Google Maps cannot fix.

## Will a travel eSIM bring Google back?

**Usually, yes.** A travel eSIM from Airalo, Holafly, or Nomad routes your data through a partner network **outside** mainland China, so Google Maps, Gmail, and WhatsApp frequently keep working with no separate VPN. That is the main reason travelers buy an eSIM rather than a local SIM, see [Best eSIM for China](/guides/best-esim-for-china/) and [eSIM vs VPN vs roaming](/guides/esim-vs-vpn-vs-roaming/).

Two caveats:

1. **Coverage varies.** Some providers and some plans route differently.
2. **Even working Google Maps has the offset problem.** Use Amap for anything where the exact entrance matters.

## Practical navigation setup

1. Install **Amap** and **Baidu Maps** at home, before you fly, see the [China apps checklist](/guides/china-apps-checklist/).
2. Download an **offline map area** for each city you will visit.
3. Save your hotel's **Chinese address** as a screenshot. It works for taxis and DiDi.
4. Keep a **translation app with camera mode** for signs and menus.

## Bottom line

Treat Google Maps as unreliable in China and Amap as your default. If you want Google services back for messaging and email, a travel eSIM is the simplest route. Read the [eSIM setup guide](/guides/esim-setup-before-you-land/) before you fly.
