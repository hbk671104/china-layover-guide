## The short answer

Shanghai is the easiest big-city layover in China. With **8 hours or more you can comfortably see The Bund and eat a real meal**; with 12 hours you get a full classic day; with 24 hours you can add a water town or the French Concession. With under 6 hours, stay at the airport.

## Which airport, and how long to reach the city

| Airport | To the center | Cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pudong (PVG) — Maglev + Metro Line 2 | ~40–55 min | ¥50 + ¥4 (¥40 with same-day boarding pass) | The fastest route in |
| Pudong (PVG) — Metro Line 2 direct | 60–90 min | ¥7–9 | Cheapest, or if your hotel is in Pudong |
| Hongqiao (SHA) — Metro Lines 2 / 10 | 30–50 min | ¥3–7 | The easiest option by far |

Pudong is roughly 30 km east of the center, so the transfer is the whole game. The Maglev covers its 30 km leg in about 8 minutes; you then transfer to Metro Line 2 at Longyang Road. Metro Line 2 also runs the entire way from Pudong to Hongqiao, but it stops 20+ times.

**A hard limit to plan around:** the Maglev stops running around 21:40 and Metro Line 2 around 22:30. Late arrivals are taxi or DiDi territory.

## What each window actually gives you

### 6 hours — not worth leaving
By the time you clear immigration (45–90 minutes is normal), ride in, and turn around, you have almost no city time. Stay airside or in the terminal, and save the city for a longer stop.

### 8 hours — The Bund and one good meal
Roughly **3–3.5 usable hours**. Take the Maglev plus Metro Line 2 to Nanjing East Road, walk to The Bund for the skyline, eat xiaolongbao (soup dumplings), and head back with a 2.5-hour buffer before your international departure.

### 12 hours — the classic day
About **6 usable hours**. Add Yu Garden and the old town, walk part of Nanjing Road, and if time allows cross to Lujiazui for the view back at the Bund. Start with the Bund before the crowds, and finish with dinner.

### 24 hours — a real stopover
Book a hotel in Puxi (People's Square, Jing'an, or the French Concession), see the classic sights unhurried, add a river cruise at night, and use the morning for a day trip — Zhujiajiao water town or a one-hour high-speed train to Hangzhou.

## The route that works

1. Land and clear immigration; collect your bags if you have them.
2. Store luggage at the airport if you're not checking into a hotel (left-luggage is usually ¥15–30 per bag).
3. Maglev + Metro Line 2 (from Pudong) or Metro Line 2/10 (from Hongqiao) to the center.
4. Bund → Yu Garden → Nanjing Road, in that order.
5. Return with 2.5–3 hours before departure if you're checking a bag.

## Before you go

- **Set up Alipay before you fly** — most small vendors and street stalls are QR-only. See [payments in China](/guides/payments-in-china-for-foreigners/).
- **Install an eSIM before you land** — airport Wi-Fi in China usually needs an SMS code you may not receive. See [eSIM setup](/guides/esim-setup-before-you-land/).
- **Have your hotel address in Chinese characters** saved on your phone if you plan to take a taxi.
- **Confirm your transit eligibility** first — see [can you leave the airport during a China layover?](/guides/can-you-leave-the-airport-during-a-china-layover/).

## Bottom line

Shanghai rewards 8 hours or more. Choose Hongqiao if you can, take the Maglev from Pudong if you can't, and treat any window under 6 hours as an airport day.
