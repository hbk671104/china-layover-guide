## The quick recommendation

**For a short China layover, buy a 3–5 GB travel eSIM before you fly — most people will be happiest with Airalo for price and reliability, or Holafly if you want unlimited data and built-in VPN routing without thinking about it.**

The single most important fact first: **China's network blocks Google, WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and sometimes Gmail.** A travel eSIM is the fix that requires no VPN app and no technical know-how — because your data is routed through a partner network *outside* mainland China, the blocked apps just keep working.

## Why the stakes are higher for layovers

A layover traveler has exactly one shot to get this right. Airport Wi-Fi in China usually demands an SMS code your home number may not receive — so if your eSIM isn't installed before you land, you're navigating a strange city with no maps, no translation, and no way to reach anyone. Buy it at home, install it on home Wi-Fi, test it before you take off. That one habit matters more than *which* provider you pick.

## Which one should you pick?

**Buy Airalo if:** you want the best balance of price and reliability, and you're comfortable managing a data allowance. The dual-network coverage (China Unicom + China Mobile) is genuinely more reliable when you're moving between cities. 3–5 GB is the right size for a layover and will cover maps, messaging, and light media.

**Buy Holafly if:** you don't want to think about data caps or VPN setup, and you're willing to pay a premium for peace of mind. Unlimited data plus built-in VPN routing is the simplest possible setup — zero decisions after install. You pay roughly 1.5–2x the cost of equivalent fixed data, but for a short trip the absolute difference is small.

**Buy Nomad if:** you're budget-sensitive and your usage is light and predictable. Nomad's fixed-data plans often start at the lowest price per trip. The trade-off is single-network coverage and fewer hand-holding features.

## How much data do you actually need?

For a layover of up to 48 hours, with maps, translation, photos, and video calls:

- **1 GB/day** is a comfortable rule of thumb.
- A **3 GB plan** covers a full-day layover with room to spare.
- A **5 GB plan** covers a multi-day stopover or heavy WhatsApp-video use.

Heavy streaming in China on unlimited plans can still hit fair-use throttling (Holafly throttles after ~90 GB/month — you'd have to be streaming constantly to hit it).

## Five things to check before you buy

1. **Your phone is unlocked and eSIM-capable.** This kills more plans than anything else.
2. **The plan routes outside mainland China.** This is *the* feature that keeps Google and WhatsApp working. All three providers above do this — but not every eSIM on the market does.
3. **Activation timing.** "Starts on install" vs "starts on first connection" differs by provider. Know which one you're buying.
4. **Refund policy.** Holafly is generous (6 months, but connection problems must be raised *during* the trip); Nomad is 30 days if never activated. Understand yours before you pay.
5. **Hotspot limits.** Holafly caps hotspot at 500 MB/day. If you plan to tether a laptop, check first.

## The step-by-step (works for any provider)

1. **Buy the eSIM at home** and install it on reliable Wi-Fi. You'll get a QR code.
2. **Name the plan** in your phone settings (e.g. "China travel").
3. **Turn off your home SIM** or disable data roaming on it to avoid surprise charges.
4. **Enable the travel eSIM** right before landing — many plans activate on first connection.
5. **Test before you leave the airport:** open Google Maps and WhatsApp in the terminal.

## Bottom line

For a China layover, the provider decision is secondary to the habit: **buy it early, install it early, test it before you take off.** If you want to stop thinking about it, buy Airalo's 3–5 GB plan for the best balance, or Holafly for unlimited data with zero setup friction. Both keep your everyday apps working the moment you land — which is the whole point.
