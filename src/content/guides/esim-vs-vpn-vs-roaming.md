## The short answer

**For a layover, a travel eSIM wins.** It is cheaper than roaming, needs no VPN, and keeps your usual apps working. Roaming is the easiest if money is no object. A VPN is only essential if you plan to use a local Chinese SIM.

## Side by side

| | Travel eSIM | VPN | Home-carrier roaming |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Setup** | Install before you fly | Install and test before you fly | Nothing to do |
| **Keeps Google/WhatsApp working** | Usually yes (routes outside China) | Yes, if it works | Often yes |
| **Cost** | Low — a few dollars per GB | Subscription on top of data | Highest — often per-day fees |
| **Reliability in China** | High | Variable; can be throttled or blocked | High |
| **Needs a VPN too?** | No | It *is* the VPN | No |
| **Best for** | Most travelers | Local-SIM users, longer stays | Very short trips, one-app users |

## How each one works

### Travel eSIM
Your data is routed through a partner network **outside mainland China**, so the Great Firewall is not in the path. That is why Google Maps, Gmail, and WhatsApp usually just work. Plans are typically 1–20 GB; 3–5 GB is plenty for a layover. See [Best eSIM for China](/guides/best-esim-for-china/).

### VPN
A VPN tunnels your traffic out of China. It works, but it can be unstable — providers get throttled, and some are blocked entirely. **The critical rule: install and test it before you fly.** VPN provider websites and app stores are hard to reach from inside China.

### Roaming
Your home carrier's roaming agreement often routes through a foreign network, so blocked apps may keep working with zero setup. The problem is price: daily roaming passes commonly cost more per day than a travel eSIM costs for the whole trip.

## The combinations that make sense

- **Short layover, want it simple:** travel eSIM only.
- **Local SIM for a cheap Chinese number:** local SIM **+** a pre-installed VPN.
- **Multi-country trip:** a regional eSIM covering China plus your next countries.
- **Emergency backup:** keep roaming enabled on your home SIM but data-off, so you can receive SMS codes.

## The rule that matters most

**Whatever you choose, set it up at home.** Airport Wi-Fi in China usually requires an SMS code your home number may not receive, and app stores behave differently once you are on a Chinese network. Install before you fly — see [China eSIM setup](/guides/esim-setup-before-you-land/) and the [apps checklist](/guides/china-apps-checklist/).

## Bottom line

Buy a travel eSIM for the trip, skip the VPN unless you are using a local SIM, and treat roaming as a backup for SMS codes rather than your data plan. For providers, see [Best eSIM for China](/guides/best-esim-for-china/).
