## The short answer

China runs on QR codes, so **Alipay or WeChat Pay is your primary method**, but carry **¥200–500 in small notes** as backup. Cash is legally required to be accepted, and it covers the two situations apps fail: vendors with personal QR codes, and a dead phone or blocked card.

## Why cash still matters

- **Personal QR codes.** Many street stalls show a personal code that cannot take a foreign-linked card. Cash settles it instantly.
- **Small denominations.** Vendors may not have change for ¥100. Notes of ¥10, ¥20, and ¥50 are far more useful.
- **Phone problems.** A dead battery, a lost signal, or a bank fraud flag can leave you with no way to pay.
- **Rural and older vendors.** Outside the big cities, more places still expect or appreciate cash.

## How much to carry

| Situation | Suggested cash |
|---|---|
| City layover, apps set up | ¥200–500 |
| Multi-city or rural travel | ¥500–1,000 |
| Emergency reserve (keep separate) | ¥100–200 |

Do not carry large amounts. The point is a fallback, not a wallet.

## Where to get yuan

- **ATMs at major banks**, Bank of China, ICBC, China Construction Bank, and Agricultural Bank accept many international cards. Withdraw a sensible amount to limit per-withdrawal fees.
- **Bank counters**, reliable, with a passport.
- **Avoid** relying on airport currency exchange; rates are usually poor compared with an ATM.

Watch for your own bank's foreign-transaction and ATM fees, and remember that if an ATM asks for a six-digit PIN and you have a four-digit one, you usually enter your four digits and press Enter.

## The rule to remember

Cash in China is **the backup, not the plan**. Set up a mobile payment app before you land ([Alipay](/guides/alipay-for-foreigners/) and [WeChat Pay](/guides/wechat-pay-and-tenpaygo-for-foreigners/)), then treat cash as insurance. If a payment fails, try the merchant's business code, switch apps, and only then reach for notes.

## Bottom line

Bring some cash, keep it in small notes, and never depend on it alone. The traveler who has both a working QR app and ¥300 in their pocket never gets stuck.
