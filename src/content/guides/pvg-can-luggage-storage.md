## The short answer

Both airports have left-luggage counters, and both charge roughly the same. What differs is **where the counter is relative to your gate, and how much time you lose getting back to it**. That is the part worth planning, and it is what this page covers. For the general rules on how Chinese airport storage works, see [luggage storage at China's airports](/guides/luggage-storage-at-china-airports/).

## PVG (Shanghai Pudong)

- **Where:** left-luggage counters in the arrivals and departures halls, signed in English as "Left Luggage" or "Baggage Storage".
- **Cost:** around ¥15–30 per bag per day, indicative.
- **What you need:** passport, and sometimes your onward ticket.

**The PVG-specific timing trap:** the Maglev stops running around 21:40 and Metro Line 2 around 22:30. If you are returning late, your trip back becomes a taxi or DiDi, which takes longer than the Maglev. Collect your bags earlier than you think you need to.

**Terminal note:** PVG has two terminals connected by a walkway and the inter-terminal train. Confirm which terminal your onward flight departs from before you decide where to store.

## CAN (Guangzhou Baiyun)

- **Where:** left-luggage counters in the terminal, on the arrivals level.
- **Cost:** around ¥15–30 per bag per day, indicative.
- **What you need:** passport.

**The CAN-specific timing trap:** you are coming back on Metro Line 3, which takes 45–55 minutes. Combined with a 2.5-hour departure buffer, that is most of your return leg. Budget the storage detour as part of it rather than on top.

**Terminal note:** Baiyun operates two terminals. Check which one your flight uses, since the walk between them is not trivial.

## The shortcut that beats both counters

If you are hiring a **private driver or taking DiDi for the day**, common for a Guangzhou dim sum run or a day in Shanghai, the driver waits with the car. Your bags stay in the vehicle and you skip the storage step at both ends.

## The airports where this matters most

Storage is a bigger deal at airports that are far from the city, because the trip back is long enough that you want to travel light:

- **PVG** is about 30 km east of Shanghai's centre.
- **CAN** is about 28 km north of Guangzhou's centre.
- Both are covered in the [airport guides](/airports/), which break down transfer times by option.

## Before you go

- **Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay**: the counters take QR payment, and you may not want to break a large note. See [payments in China](/guides/payments-in-china-for-foreigners/).
- **Keep documents on you**: passport, boarding pass and onward ticket details never go in stored luggage.
- **Photograph your bag and the tag**.

## Bottom line

The counters at PVG and CAN are straightforward. The planning that matters is **the return leg**: at PVG because the Maglev stops early, at CAN because the metro ride is long. If you have a car, skip storage entirely.
