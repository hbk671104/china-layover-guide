## The short answer

China's visa-free landscape is not just two lists. There are also **regional schemes** (most notably **30 days in Hainan** for 59+ countries) and **bilateral agreements** with around 29 countries. If a scheme applies to you, it can be simpler than the transit rules — but each has its own area, purpose, and stay limits.

## The main schemes

| Scheme | Scope | Stay | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unilateral 30-day entry** | Nationwide | 30 days | ~50 countries; no third-country rule |
| **240-hour transit** | 65 ports, 24 provinces | 240 hours | 55 countries; third-country ticket required |
| **Hainan 30-day** | Hainan Province only | 30 days | 59+ countries; tourism, business, family, medical, conferences, sports |
| **Bilateral agreements** | Nationwide | Typically 30 days per entry, 90 days per 180 | ~29 countries |
| **Hong Kong / Macao group tours** | Specific routes | Varies | Tourist-group conditions apply |

## Hainan: 30 days without a visa

Hainan runs its own visa-free policy, separate from the national rules:

- **Who:** ordinary passport holders from 59+ countries
- **How long:** up to **30 days**
- **Purpose:** tourism, business, visits, family, medical treatment, conferences and exhibitions, sports competitions
- **Not allowed:** work or study
- **Where:** within Hainan Province; entry through all open ports in Hainan

Because Hainan's list is broader than the national 30-day list, some travelers who do not qualify nationally can still enter Hainan visa-free. If your trip is a beach stopover, check this route.

## Bilateral agreements

China has mutual visa-exemption agreements with around **29 countries**, including Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, the UAE, Qatar, Kazakhstan, and several others. The typical structure is a stay of up to **30 days per entry, with a cumulative cap within a 180-day period**.

Where a bilateral agreement exists, **it takes precedence** over the general unilateral scheme — and it may be more generous. Always check whether your country has a bilateral deal before assuming the 30-day unilateral list is your only option.

## How to check which applies

1. **Find your nationality** on the [NIA unilateral list](https://en.nia.gov.cn/n147418/n147468/c182992/content.html) and the [240-hour transit list](/guides/240-hour-transit-ports-and-permitted-areas/).
2. **Check for a bilateral agreement** — these are usually the most flexible.
3. **If your destination is Hainan**, check the regional scheme separately.
4. **Confirm with the airline** before booking non-refundable travel; they check at boarding.

## Important caveats

- **Schemes change and are renewed in waves.** A country on a list today may not be next year.
- **Purpose matters.** Tourism, business, and family visits are covered; work, study, and journalism are not.
- **Do not stack schemes creatively.** Trying to route around the rules is a common cause of refusal.
- **The scheme must match your actual itinerary**, not just your passport.

## Bottom line

Before assuming you need a visa, check three things: the **national 30-day list**, the **240-hour transit rule**, and any **bilateral agreement**. If your destination is Hainan, check that scheme too. For the national picture, start with [30-day visa-free entry vs 240-hour transit](/guides/30-day-visa-free-vs-240-hour-transit/).
