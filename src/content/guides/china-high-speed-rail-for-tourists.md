## The short answer

China's high-speed rail is fast, punctual, and easy for foreigners — you book with your **passport** as the ID and board by scanning it at the gate. Book through **Trip.com** if you want English and a foreign card, or the official **12306** app if you want the source of truth. Arrive **45–60 minutes early**; stations are big and security is mandatory.

## Why it matters for a layover

High-speed rail turns a stopover into a real trip. From Shanghai you can reach **Hangzhou** (West Lake) in about an hour. From Beijing, **Xi'an** is roughly 4.5 hours — feasible inside a 240-hour transit window. Within the transit rules you must still respect the **permitted areas** and exit through a **designated port** — see the [ports and permitted areas reference](/guides/240-hour-transit-ports-and-permitted-areas/).

## How to book

| Method | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Trip.com** | English interface, foreign cards, easy refunds | Small booking fee |
| **12306 (official)** | Cheapest, definitive | Clunkier for foreign cards, Chinese-first UX |
| **Station counter** | Works when nothing else does | Queue, language barrier |

Both online options require **your passport details**. Book a few days ahead for popular routes and holidays — the good departures sell out.

## Seat classes

| Class | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Second class** | Perfectly comfortable; the default choice |
| **First class** | Wider seats, quieter carriage |
| **Business class** | Lie-flat, expensive, rarely worth it for a day trip |

## At the station

1. **Arrive 45–60 minutes early.** Major stations are the size of airports.
2. **Security screening** is mandatory for all bags.
3. **Scan your passport** at the gate — e-tickets mean no paper ticket is needed.
4. **Find your carriage and seat** on the platform displays.
5. **Boarding closes shortly before departure** — do not cut it fine.

## Practical tips

- **Your passport is your ticket.** Keep it accessible, not buried in a bag.
- **Note your train number** (e.g. G1234) — that is what signage and staff use.
- **Store bags in the racks** at the carriage ends; overhead space is limited.
- **Food:** a trolley and sometimes a dining car, but bring your own for short hops.
- **Same-day returns** are easy on short routes like Shanghai–Hangzhou.
- **Do not plan tight rail connections before an international flight** — leave a real buffer.

## Fares to expect

Indicative second-class fares:

| Route | Time | Fare |
|---|---|---|
| Shanghai → Hangzhou | ~1 hour | from ~¥73 |
| Beijing → Xi'an | ~4.5 hours | ~¥515+ |
| Guangzhou → Shenzhen | ~35 min | ~¥75 |
| Chengdu → Chongqing | ~1.5 hours | ~¥154 |

Fares and schedules change — check 12306 or Trip.com before you plan around them.

## Bottom line

High-speed rail is one of the best things about traveling in China and it works smoothly for foreigners. Book with your passport, arrive early, and use it to extend a layover into a genuine trip. Pair it with [DiDi](/guides/didi-and-ride-hailing-in-china/) for the last mile to the station.
